A Sum-Product Problem

The article—or at least the part that I could make sense of—is about the opposite of Erdos’s Sum-Product Problem. This article explores questions about the greatest number of different values in the corresponding addition and multiplication tables for different sequences of positive integers.

Jean-Paul’s sequence, A337655, is the lexicographically earliest (greedy) sequence such that for all n both the addition and multiplication tables of the first n terms have (n+12) distinct terms (the greatest number).

The addition and multiplication tables share some values. For example, they both have a 2, a 4, a 7, a 10, and so on. My sequence, A337946, is similar to Jean-Paul’s but with the additional restriction that the addition and multiplication tables cannot have any values in common.

Some related sequences

I’ve recently added some new sequences to the OEIS related to these sequences.

One way of thinking of A066720 is that it’s the lexicographically earliest infinite sequence S=\(ai_{i=1}^{\infty}\) such that for all n the set Sn={ai}i=1n consisting of the first n terms of S has the property that Sn×Sn={xy∣x,y∈Sn} has exactly (n+12) elements—the most possible.

A347498: minimize the largest term

If instead of minimizing the lexicographic order, we instead minimize the size of the largest element, we get OEIS sequence A347498: for each n, find the least m such that there exists a subset Tn⊆{1,2,…,m} with n elements such that |Tn×Tn|=(n+12). A347498(n) records the value of the ms.

A347570: Sums with more terms

Perhaps instead of asking when all sums x+y are distinct for x≤y, we want to know when all sums x1+x2+⋯+xn are distinct for x1≤x2≤⋯≤xn. This family of sequences are sometimes called Bn sequences and have been studied by the likes of Richard Guy.

I’ve recorded the lexicographically earliest Bn sequences in OEIS sequence A347570.
