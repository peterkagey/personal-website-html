Potato printing plot party postcards

by Peter Kagey (2025-12-27)

I'm participating in the 2025 plotter postcard exchange (#ptpx 2025) which consists of 94 participants from 18 countries exchanging postcards drawn on pen plotters across the globe! (This is my second postcard exchange. You can read about my first one here!)

I decided to base my design on a game called "potato printing" that M.C. Escher played with his kids, where they would carve a potato to make a square stamp, and then stamp that pattern onto a square grid. In Doris Schattschneider's book Visions of Symmetry (pp. 44), I saw a particular tile that I used as an inspiration for my postcard.

On each postcard, I two overlapping 2×3 grids of these designs, each drawn in a different color with the property that two tiles in the same position were offset by ±90° or 180° from each other, as shown below. I designed the tile by tracing it on Inkscape, and then using a trick to convert it into a series of concentric polygons. The trick involves copying the polygon many times, pasting it on top of itself with varying stroke thicknesses, and then using "Stroke to Path" to get the concentric effect. Once the tile was designed, I used svg.py to randomize the tiles and fill in two overlapping grids.
Every postcard has a different random arrangement of tile orientations, and most have different color choices, as shown in the following two examples.

Potato Printing

I learned about the "potato printing" game from Doris Schattschneider's book, but M.C. Escher's son George Escher wrote about it for the International Congress on M.C. Escher which took place in Rome in 1985 . An illustration that follows George's description contains illustrations that are awfully postcard-like, and I like to think that Escher might have sent out postcards like this if he were to have participated in #ptpx 2025. I've vectorized the tile design used in these illustrations, in case you want to use the pen plotter (or just a printer) to make some of these postcards for yourself.

The Magic Mirror of M.C. Escher

You can read more about this, read a quote from M.C. Escher, and see colored in versions of some of these tilings in The Magic Mirror of M.C. Escher by Bruno Ernst, published in 1976. Here are some colorized versions of the resulting tilings.
