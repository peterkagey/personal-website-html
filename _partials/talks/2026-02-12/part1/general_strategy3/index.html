With these vertices, we can explicitly compute how each \(g \in G\) acts on the faces and detect when and how "faces land on top of themselves" under repeated application of \(g \in G\). That is, we can compute when the size of an orbit of a face under the action of a cyclic subgroup \(\langle g \rangle\) is less than the order of \(g\).
When the size of the orbit is the order of the cyclic subgroup, we can freely choose our tile for that orbit. When it is smaller, we must choose an appropriately symmetric tile for that position, where the symmetry respects how the faces "land on top of themselves."
