In 1957, Martin Gardner popularized a series of pentominoes packing puzzles in his "Mathematical Games" column in Scientific American, which he attributed to Sol Golomb (USC).
"It is also possible to fit the 12 pentominoes to form rectangles that are 6 × 10, 5 × 12, 4 × 15 and 3 × 20. [...] The 3 × 20 rectangle, by all odds the most difficult, is left for the interested reader to construct."
—Martin Gardner
