Ron Graham's sequence (A006255) is the least k for which there exists a strictly increasing sequence n=b1<b2<⋯<bt=k where ∏i=1tbi is a square.

There is a known way to efficiently compute analogous functions ap where ap(n) is the least integer such that there exists a sequence

  1. n=b1≤b2≤⋯≤bt=ap(n),
  2. any term appears at most p−1 times, and
  3. ∏i=1tbi is a p-th power.

Question

An efficient way to compute ap is known when p is prime. What is an efficient way to compute ac when c is composite?

Related questions

  1. For what values n is a4(n)< A006255(n)?
  2. Given integers k,c, how many n satisfy ac(n)=k? (e.g. a4(6)=a4(9)=9.)
  3. Does ac contain arbitrarily many copies of the same value? That is, do there exist arbitrarily large m and distinct n1,…,nm with a4(n1)=⋯=a4(nm)?
  4. How many times does k appear in the image of ac? (e.g. 9 appears twice, as a4(6) and a4(9).)
  5. Which integers occur in the image of ac?
