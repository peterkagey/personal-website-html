← Problem 79 →

Suppose you have two finite groups \(G\) and \(H\), and an \(H\)-set \(\Omega\). Denote the base of the wreath product of \(G\) by \(H\) as \(K = \prod_{\omega \in \Omega} G_\omega\).

We are interested in determining when it is possible to construct a finite sequence \(\{k_i \in K\}_{i=1}^N\) such that for any choice of \(\alpha \in G \wr_\Omega H\) and any sequence \(\{h_i \in H\}_{i=1}^N\), there exists \(n \le N\) satisfying \[ \alpha \cdot (k_1, h_1) \cdot (k_2, h_2) \cdots (k_n, h_n) = (e_K, h) \] for some \(h \in H\).

Question

What conditions on \(G \wr_\Omega H\) guarantee the existence of such a finite sequence of elements in \(K\)?

Related questions

  1. Given \(G \wr_\Omega H\), what is \(N\), the minimum length of the sequence?
  2. If both \(\alpha\) and the sequence \(\{h_i \in H\}_{i=1}^N\) are chosen uniformly at random, what is the expected value of the minimal \(n\) such that \(\alpha \cdot (k_1, h_1) \cdot (k_2, h_2) \cdot \dots \cdot (k_n, h_n) = (e_K, h)\)?
  3. What if there is a set of elements in the base, any one of which is valid (for example, all base elements with the number of "heads" congruent to \(2 \pmod 3\))?
  4. What if some information about \(s_i\) is revealed after the \(i\)th move and the strategy may depend on this information?

Notes

This problem appears in Peter Winkler's book under the name "Spinning Switches". It generalizes to \(H=C_{2^n}\) acting on a \(2^n\)-gon (but not to other \(m\)-gons). One can show (1) it is impossible for odd \(m\), and (2) solvability for an \(m\)-gon is equivalent to solvability for every proper divisor \(d\mid m\). Allowing coins with three states yields solvability on \(3^n\)-gons.
