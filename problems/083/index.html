Consider a system of first-order finite difference equations (linear recurrences) a1,a2,…,aN where

ai(n)=αi1a1(n−1)+αi2a2(n−1)+⋯+αiNaN(n−1).

Question

Given some linear combination a(n)=k1ai1(n)+k2ai2(n)+⋯+kmaim(n) of these finite difference sequences, what is the smallest-order finite difference equation that a(n) satisfies?

Related questions

  1. How does the order of such a recurrence depend on the initial conditions of the system?
  2. What if the initial recurrences have order greater than 1? For example, if each ai(n) depends on terms aj(n−1),…,aj(n−kj), how does that affect the order of a recurrence satisfied by linear combinations?

Note

Let αij be the coefficient of aj(n−1) in the finite difference equation for ai(n), and let A=[αij]i,j=1N. If the characteristic/minimal polynomial of A is det(xI−A)=xm+βm−1xm−1+⋯+β1x+β0, then any such linear combination a(n) satisfies a(n)=−βm−1a(n−1)−βm−2a(n−2)−⋯−β1a(n−m+1)−β0a(n−m). However, in particular cases a lower-order recurrence may hold.
