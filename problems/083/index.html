← Problem 83 →

Consider a system of first-order finite difference equations (linear recurrences) \(a_1, a_2, \dots, a_N\) where

\(a_i(n) = \alpha_{i1}a_1(n-1) + \alpha_{i2}a_2(n-1) + \dots + \alpha_{iN}a_N(n-1).\)

Question

Given some linear combination \(a(n)=k_1a_{i_1}(n)+k_2a_{i_2}(n)+\dots+k_ma_{i_m}(n)\) of these finite difference sequences, what is the smallest-order finite difference equation that \(a(n)\) satisfies?

Related questions

  1. How does the order of such a recurrence depend on the initial conditions of the system?
  2. What if the initial recurrences have order greater than 1? For example, if each \(a_i(n)\) depends on terms \(a_j(n-1),\dots,a_j(n-k_j)\), how does that affect the order of a recurrence satisfied by linear combinations?

Note

Let \(\alpha_{ij}\) be the coefficient of \(a_j(n-1)\) in the finite difference equation for \(a_i(n)\), and let \(A=[\alpha_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^N\). If the characteristic/minimal polynomial of \(A\) is \(\det(xI-A)=x^m+\beta_{m-1}x^{m-1}+\dots+\beta_1x+\beta_0\), then any such linear combination \(a(n)\) satisfies \[ a(n) = -\beta_{m-1}a(n-1)-\beta_{m-2}a(n-2)-\dots-\beta_1a(n-m+1)-\beta_0a(n-m). \] However, in particular cases a lower-order recurrence may hold.
